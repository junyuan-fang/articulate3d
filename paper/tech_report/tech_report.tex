\begin{abstract}
Articulate3D evaluates scene-level movable-part articulation and
interaction-element segmentation~\cite{halacheva2025articulate3d}. Starting
from released USDNet checkpoints, we combine geometric refinement and
structural mask splitting with later full-network flip finetuning and
ensembling. Final test scores are $0.4056$ on the movable ranking metric and
$0.3286$ AP$_{50}$ on interaction, placing second on both test leaderboards.
Validation is in-sample. Test ground-truth annotations were not accessed
directly, but server scores and unlabeled test predictions informed
development.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Method}

Movable-part matches require mask IoU above $0.5$, sign-invariant axis error below $15^\circ$, and (for rotations) origin-to-axis-line distance below $0.25$\,m. The interaction metric is mask AP$_{50}$. Baseline errors show axis regression collapsing toward gravity and origins shrinking toward part centroids.

\begingroup\small
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Initial frozen segmentation.} Minkowski U-Net backbone~\cite{choy2019minkowski}, Mask3D decoder~\cite{schult2023mask3d}, mask head and class head are kept at the released weights, \emph{including BatchNorm running statistics}. \apfifty{} is therefore bit-identical to the baseline within a checkpoint for articulation-only edits. Structural splitting and later full-network finetuning explicitly change the mask predictions.
\item \textbf{One trained component.} An 88\,K-parameter attention-pooled origin head (scoring MLP plus origin MLP) is finetuned with the rest of the network frozen. The comparison measures this changed head under a frozen-backbone setup; it does not isolate attention aggregation alone. Two implementation issues affected early runs: (a) BatchNorm buffers can drift during training and confound validation comparisons even when parameters are frozen; (b) resuming a released checkpoint restarts USDNet's coarse-to-fine mask curriculum, which dilates the GT masks by 0.1\,m for 100 epochs and caused severe training degradation unless disabled.
\item \textbf{Training-free geometric refinement.} Translation axes use the predicted mask's PCA panel normal, or an in-plane direction when a sliding partner leaf exists; rotation axes are forced vertical. Rotation origins are snapped to a mask endpoint perpendicular to the axis. If a predicted handle is near the part, the endpoint farther from it is selected; otherwise the endpoint nearest the model's predicted origin is used. Handles come from the companion interactable-part model. The initial head-plus-geometry recipe takes test \metric{} from $0.264$ to $0.3385$ with the segmentation branch untouched.
\item \textbf{Structural post-processing (both tasks).} The model routinely merges the two leaves of a double door into one instance. Offline DBSCAN~\cite{ester1996dbscan} connected-component splitting $+$ fragment filter $+$ NMS, tuned with the official evaluator on validation, improves test scores (movable $0.3385\!\to\!0.3760$; interaction $0.2686\!\to\!0.2886$).
\item \textbf{Full-network finetuning and ensemble.} Later train$+$validation finetunes update the full network with flip augmentation and the curriculum disabled. Interaction: the \texttt{v5}$+$\texttt{v6} union reaches $0.3240$; adding \texttt{v5b} gives $0.3286$. Movable: a single \texttt{mov\_v11} checkpoint with geometric and structural refinement gives $0.4056$. Validation is in-sample; these cumulative competition gains are not controlled multi-seed ablations.
\end{enumerate}
\endgroup
